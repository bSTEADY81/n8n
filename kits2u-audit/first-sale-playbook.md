# Kits2u.au — First Sale Playbook

## The honest starting point

You're selling a **$1,800–$6,000+ considered purchase** from a brand nobody has heard of, on a site Google hasn't indexed. Organic SEO will take 3–6 months to bring meaningful traffic. Your first sale will almost certainly come from one of these instead:

1. **A quote conversation** (form or phone) that you personally close, or
2. **A local/marketplace channel** where buyers already are.

So the playbook is: fix the site (Part 1 of `fix-checklist.md`), then go where the buyers are and pull them into a quote conversation.

---

## Week 1 — Free channels, same-day setup

### 1. Google Business Profile (highest ROI single action)
- Create at business.google.com: category "Patio Enclosure Supplier" / "Carport & Pergola Builder", service area = your delivery region.
- Add photos, hours, phone, link to kits2u.au.
- For searches like "patio kits melbourne", the local map pack appears **above** organic results — a fresh GBP can get you visibility months before SEO does.
- Ask every future customer for a Google review; at 3–5 reviews you'll start getting calls.

### 2. Facebook Marketplace + Gumtree
- List each kit individually ("Single Carport Kit — Colorbond, custom sizes, from $2,600, delivered") with your best photos, in your metro area.
- These platforms have massive intent-driven traffic for carports/patios in Australia and cost nothing.
- Answer messages fast; move buyers to a phone call, close them, and take payment through the website (bank transfer or card link) so you exercise your own checkout.

### 3. Facebook community groups
- Join local homeowner / renovation / "DIY Australia" groups. Don't spam — answer questions about patios, council approvals, Colorbond colours. One helpful answer with "I run kits2u.au, happy to quote it" converts better than any ad.

### 4. Turn the quote form into a sales machine
- Reply to **every** enquiry within 1 hour if possible (24h maximum, as the site promises).
- Template: recommendation + exact delivered price + a photo of a similar build + one question that keeps the conversation going ("Is your slab already poured?").
- Follow up every quote after 3 days and 10 days. Most first sales in this category are won in the follow-up because competitors don't bother.
- **Later, automate this with your n8n instance**: form submission → instant acknowledgment email + Telegram/SMS alert to you → automatic 3-day follow-up if no reply. (You already run n8n — this is a perfect first workflow, happy to build it.)

## Week 2–3 — Small paid tests (only after checkout works)

### 5. Google Ads — one tightly-scoped Search campaign
- Budget: $15–25/day. Keywords (phrase match): "patio kits", "carport kits", "diy patio kit", "insulated patio kit" + your city/state.
- Negative keywords: "bunnings", "second hand", "used".
- Send clicks to the relevant **product page**, not the homepage. Track add-to-carts and quote-form submits in GA4.
- At your margins, one sale per 100–200 clicks pays for the campaign.

### 6. Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads — optional second test
- Carousel of finished patios → "Get a free quote in 24h" lead form. Works better for patios (aspirational) than carports (functional).

## Ongoing — compounding assets

### 7. Content that matches how Australians buy this product
Your blog already has a decent start ("Carports vs. Garages"). Add, one per fortnight:
- "Do I need council approval for a patio/carport in [state]?" ← highest-intent question in the niche
- "Flyover vs fascia-mounted patio: which suits your roofline?"
- "What does a DIY patio kit really cost in 2026?" (with a real price table — price transparency is your edge against quote-only competitors)
- Each post ends with the quote CTA.

### 8. Reviews and proof
- After each of your first sales: ask for a Google review + 3 photos of the finished build (offer a $50 fuel card if needed). Put them on the homepage and product pages.
- Document your own/first installs as photo + short video. Real content replaces the competitor photos currently in the gallery.

### 9. The "supply & install" wedge
You already have a Supply & Install page. Many buyers *want* a kit price but *fear* the install. Partner with 1–2 local installers (Hipages/ServiceSeeking or trade contacts), add "DIY or installed — your choice" to product pages, and you widen the market dramatically.

## What "success" looks like in 30 days

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Checkout functional (test order placed) | Week 1 |
| Google Business Profile live | Week 1 |
| Site indexed (`site:kits2u.au` shows pages) | Week 2–3 |
| Quote enquiries | 5–10 |
| Marketplace conversations | 10+ |
| **First sale** | Week 2–4, most likely from a quote follow-up or Marketplace conversation |

The single most important habit: **treat every enquiry like it's the first sale** — because one of them will be.
